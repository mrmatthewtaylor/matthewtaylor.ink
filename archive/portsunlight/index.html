About the Collection

Port Sunlight unfolds as an exceptional living architectural archive, capturing the visionary ideals of late 19th-century industrial housing through its picturesque arrangement of timber-framed black-and-white gables, robust brick terraces, and serene open greens. The village showcases an extraordinary stylistic diversity across its residential and public buildings—spanning from vernacular Tudor and Jacobean Revival motifs featuring elaborate decorative brick chimneys and mullioned windows to restrained Georgian classical composition complete with formal symmetry and ornamental cornices. Framed by mature autumnal trees and connected by stone-paved pathways, these meticulously preserved model dwellings and civic landmarks reflect an enduring commitment to craftsmanship, domestic comfort, and harmonious urban design.

About This Project

For me, classical architecture has always been a language built on proportion, geometry, and rigor. In this series, I explore that syntax through the lens of spoliation, taking historical fragments like Mannerist volutes and Renaissance pediments and reimagining them within new digital compositions.
Instead of rigid replication, I rely on precise vector draftsmanship to bring disparate motifs together, balancing clean stroke hierarchies to honour traditional building forms while pushing their boundaries into something entirely my own.
Every single vector in this project is drawn by hand. I believe deeply in authentic human craft; no artificial intelligence has been used to generate or synthesise any part of these illustrations.
