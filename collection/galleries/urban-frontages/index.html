Urban Frontages: Gallery

Multi-Storey Facades & Streetfronts

Classical Commercial Elevation with Colonaded Shopfront

An orthographic line drawing of a two-storey commercial facade (A.J. Perry & Sons). The ground floor features a multi-column colonnade framing shopfront glazing and an entry portal, while the upper brick elevation displays four sash windows with stone keystone lintels beneath a pedimented roofline.

Arched Storefront Elevation with Balustrade parapet

A vector elevation of a commercial building elevation showcasing a prominent ground-floor segment arch glazed window above a inset entrance door. The upper story is accented by five pedimented sash windows set against brickwork, topped by a detailed balustraded parapet and central gable plaque.

Civic Renaissance Revival Facade with Clock Tower

A three-storey orthographic elevation depicting a formal civic building with an offset entrance bay. The composition incorporates a classical pedimented portico entry, layered window pediments across all levels, a roof balustrade, and a multi-tiered clock tower/cupola crown.

About this collection

An orthographic vector series exploring traditional multi-storey frontages and streetfront compositions. Drawing on classical and revivalist architectural traditions, these detailed line studies examine structural joinery, Classical orders, fenestration rhythms, and ground-floor display openings to catalog traditional British and Western urban design parameters.

About This Project

For me, classical architecture has always been a language built on proportion, geometry, and rigor. In this series, I explore that syntax through the lens of spoliation, taking historical fragments like Mannerist volutes and Renaissance pediments and reimagining them within new digital compositions.
Instead of rigid replication, I rely on precise vector draftsmanship to bring disparate motifs together, balancing clean stroke hierarchies to honour traditional building forms while pushing their boundaries into something entirely my own.
Every single vector in this project is drawn by hand. I believe deeply in authentic human craft; no artificial intelligence has been used to generate or synthesise any part of these illustrations.
